# PI Feature Readiness Review Criteria

Version: 0.1.0 — draft for review

This rubric is coaching-oriented. It identifies refinement opportunities before PI Planning. It is not a compliance checklist or binary gate.

## Qualitative indicators
- **Strong** — clear enough to support useful planning conversation; no material concern evident.
- **Could be clearer** — understandable, but more precision would reduce ambiguity or planning risk.
- **Needs attention** — a material gap or ambiguity is likely to make planning/execution harder.
- **Missing information** — relevant information is not available; do not infer or invent it.

## Overall guidance
- **Well Prepared** — coherent enough for productive PI Planning discussion with no material refinement theme evident.
- **Minor Refinement Suggested** — broadly understandable with a small number of useful clarifications.
- **Refinement Recommended** — several meaningful gaps or ambiguities should be addressed.
- **Significant Clarification Needed** — core aspects remain unclear, missing, or inconsistent.

Overall guidance describes refinement need, not permission to enter the PI.

## 1. Intent & Value
Review whether it is clear what should change, who benefits, why it matters, and whether the feature expresses outcome/value rather than only solution detail. Look for generic statements such as “improve”, “optimize”, “enable”, or “streamline” without meaningful context.

## 2. Scope & Clarity
Review whether the intended capability, in-scope boundaries, out-of-scope boundaries, and important terms are clear enough to support shared understanding. Look for overly broad scope, multiple unrelated outcomes, vague wording, and likely interpretation conflicts.

## 3. Acceptance
Review whether acceptance criteria exist where appropriate, describe observable/testable outcomes, align with the feature intent, and are specific enough to reduce ambiguity without becoming implementation instructions.

## 4. Ownership & Priority
Review whether an accountable owner is identifiable, a delivery team is known or intentionally TBD, and priority is stated. Do not judge whether the chosen priority is correct; judge whether the planning context is clear.

## 5. Sizing & Breakdown
Review whether an initial size exists, whether the feature seems understood enough to size, and whether there is an initial story breakdown or other evidence of decomposition. Do not require a final story backlog before PI Planning unless explicitly required by the organization.

## 6. Dependencies
Review dependencies on other features, teams, systems/platforms, vendors, decisions/approvals, infrastructure, and other external constraints. Look for what is needed, from whom/what, whether it may block delivery, and who coordinates it. When Dependency Type = Feature, use Feature IDs.

## 7. Risks & Unknowns
Identify explicit risks, assumptions presented as facts, unresolved questions, material missing information, and contradictions across fields, stories, or dependencies. Distinguish supplied facts, assumptions, agent observations, and open questions.

## Feedback style
For each dimension prefer:
- Indicator
- Observation
- Why it matters
- Recommendation
- Questions to resolve

Avoid pass/fail language, compliance framing, invented facts, and generic advice when a specific recommendation is possible.
